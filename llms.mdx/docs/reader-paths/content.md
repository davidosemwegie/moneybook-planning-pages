# Reader paths (/docs/reader-paths)



The four paths follow [Note 3](/docs/raw-thoughts/note-3). [Chapter 1](/docs/chapters/start-where-you-are) explains them in the author's voice and guides the first exercise. Readers can use the [companion workbook](/docs/workbook) or plain paper.

## The four starting points [#the-four-starting-points]

| ID   | Starting situation                                       | Next chapter after Chapter 1                                 |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| I-D  | Current income or support, with money owed.              | [When you get paid](/docs/chapters/when-you-get-paid)        |
| I-ND | Current income or support, with no money owed.           | [The life you want](/docs/chapters/the-life-you-want)        |
| N-D  | No current income or support source, with money owed.    | [Find and grow your income](/docs/chapters/grow-your-income) |
| N-ND | No current income or support source, with no money owed. | [Find and grow your income](/docs/chapters/grow-your-income) |

Give each path one next chapter. For I-D, start with incoming money before building a debt plan. Both no-income paths start with finding income. This follows the author's [direction on next steps](/docs/raw-thoughts/plan-decisions#one-next-chapter-for-each-path).

These describe a starting point. They do not measure a person's worth, savings, or whether income covers expenses. A path can change.

An ongoing source can pay at uneven times. Someone living from savings alone belongs in a no-income path for this workbook, with those savings recorded beside the path. Keep one-time receipts visible without treating them as ongoing income.

## What counts as debt [#what-counts-as-debt]

The author has defined it: **money you owe**. Include amounts owed to people, lenders, providers, and government. A mortgage and money borrowed from a friend both count. A balance does not stop being debt because it is called “good.”

Include balances already owed even when the payment is not late. Mark due dates so routine bills and overdue amounts are distinguishable. Exclude future purchases that have not happened. Record disputed or uncertain obligations separately for checking.

The goal of income without debt remains part of the author's direction. A definition alone does not settle the order or speed of repayment; those steps belong in [Chapter 5](/docs/chapters/make-a-plan-for-debt).

## Income-source labels [#income-source-labels]

Use descriptive labels instead of forcing every reader into hourly, salary, or business.

| Label                | Examples on the workbook map                                                       |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Employer             | Hourly pay, salary, tips, commissions, bonuses from employment                     |
| Own work             | Freelance work, a service, products, a business or creator channel the reader runs |
| Assets               | Interest, dividends, rent, royalties                                               |
| Support / retirement | Pensions, cash benefits, regular family support, child support                     |

Hourly and salary are pay methods within Employer. Readers can use multiple labels. A later passage could say **I-D · Employer + Own work**. Use **I-D** alone when the source does not change the step. Use **All readers** for shared material.

Sort each payment once. Keep timing, reliability, business costs, and tax still to cover beside it. Borrowing, savings withdrawals, sales, and one-time gifts have a separate cash-receipts line in Chapter 1.

## Debt details [#debt-details]

The chapter's three groups describe how the amount arose: borrowing or credit, unpaid bills, or taxes, fines, and court-ordered amounts. Then record who is owed, the balance, payment dates, interest and fees, any property pledged, and the purpose.

“Secured,” “unsecured,” “instalment,” and “revolving” describe terms to explain in Chapter 5. “Good” and “bad” describe a judgment to examine. Neither replaces the simple debt yes/no question.
