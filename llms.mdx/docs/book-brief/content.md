# Book brief (/docs/book-brief)



## What this book is [#what-this-book-is]

This is a practical book you come back to as your life changes. Start with the situation you're in. Work through the steps that fit. Come back when you need the next part.

The book helps readers define the life they want, work out what it costs, and decide what to do from where they are now. It uses facts, stories, worked examples, and exercises they can complete with their own numbers.

There are two parts: the book and an optional [companion workbook](/docs/workbook). The book explains the steps and shows examples. The workbook holds the fill-in pages. Each exercise names its workbook page and gives instructions for doing the same work on plain paper. The book must work on its own.

## Who it's for [#who-its-for]

People have different incomes, debts, priorities, and ideas of a good life. The book meets them at that starting point. It does not assume everyone wants to own a business, work for someone else, or become a millionaire.

The [reader paths](/docs/reader-paths) follow the four situations from Note 3: income with debt, income without debt, no income with debt, and no income without debt. Current income includes support. The four source labels are Employer, Own work, Assets, and Support / retirement. A reader can use more than one.

For someone with no income, the author's first priority is to get income. The larger aim is the life the reader wants. Growing income and reducing debt support that aim. Debt means money owed, including amounts someone calls good debt. Repayment decisions need more detail than that definition alone.

## The shape of the book [#the-shape-of-the-book]

The [chapter plan](/docs/chapters) has 12 chapters. Chapter 1 puts the reader's current money, income, and debt on paper and identifies their path. Seeing the numbers gives the reader a starting point for a plan. Chapter 2 puts a cost on their desired life. Later chapters cover income, paydays, debt, credit, purchases, change, quick-wealth promises, and investing.

Smaller questions belong as sections inside these chapters. Existing topic pages remain writing notes that feed the chapters.

## What each section must give the reader [#what-each-section-must-give-the-reader]

* A clear question and a plain explanation.
* A story or example when it helps explain the point.
* Steps the reader can take, including notes for the relevant reader groups.
* An exercise or decision they can finish, with a companion-workbook page and a plain-paper option.
* Sources for factual claims, figures, rules, and comparisons.
* A next page to use when their situation changes.

## The author's point of view [#the-authors-point-of-view]

The author speaks from experience while still working through his own finances. He is candid about debt, grounded in faith, and interested in helping people make progress without shaming them for their priorities.

Use [Notes 1–9](/docs/raw-thoughts) and the [writing guide](/docs/writing-style) as the voice reference. Preserve the author's questions, everyday examples, directness, and admissions when turning speech into book prose.
